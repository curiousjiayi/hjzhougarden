
# Flight training small birds

Flight training small birds is straightforward but requires patience. There are only three principles you need to remember during training:
1. Birds are smart and capable.
2. You are big and scary, and hence need to project trust.
3. Learning what this big scary hairless animal wants you to do via interspecies charades is hard, so have some empathy.

I believe that with flight training, there is no reason to clip your bird's wings. These are the steps that I used to train my two budgies for flight and recall.

---

## I. Master the Step-up

1. With your bird in your cage, offer them some tasty snack, like millet (for budgies).
2. Do this a few times over a few days.
3. Then, offer your index finger with your knuckle facing outwards towards your bird. Put your finger slowly up to your bird near their belly. Offer them millet held in your other hand, holding it so they can access the millet by leaning slightly over your finger.
4. Move the millet a bit farther away so they have to lean farther.
5. Move the millet farther away so they have to put a leg on your finger to reach it.
6. Move the millet farther away so they have to step on your finger fully.
7. Give them enthusiastic praise, like "good bird!"
8. Once they are used to stepping onto your finger, put the millet away. Offer them a finger first, and then once they step up praise them enthusiastically and give them millet. This associates stepping up with rewards.
9. Bonus: you can **clicker train** at this stage. When they step up, press a clicker (or make a clicking sound with your mouth, which is what I do). This serves as a "mission accomplished" signal with your bird so they have a definitive indicator that they did what you wanted them to do.

---

## II. Short flights

1. Flight training is best done in a room they're familiar with, and without major collision objects (e.g. windows, pillars) and tall landing spots.
2. With your bird on your cage or on a stand, ask them to step up.
3. Once they're familiar with step-up, simply step farther back and ask them to fly to you. To start. a forearm width's away is approachable.
4. If they are hesitant, you can:
	- Show millet by your finger
 	- If pointer trained (e.g. with a chopstick), show the pointer
5. Start moving farther away, and train short flights with two or three fixed points. I started asking [[snowy|Snowy]] to fly from the cage to my desk (on the other side of the room), giving him millet, and then sending him back to his cage (by lightly moving my arm away from my torso so he is sent flying off). When he gets back to the cage he'll turn around and we can train again.

--- 

## III. Make sure your bird knows how to descend

Flying down is a scary thing for a bird that hasn't tried it before. If your bird ever accidentally gets outside, they will likely fly to a tree. Training recall from a high spot (like a tree branch) to a lower spot (like you frantically standing on the ground beckoning at your bird to come down) can save your bird's life.

1. Free-flighted birds will pick tall spots around the home, so you'll have plenty of opportunity to practice. Otherwise, pick a room with a curtain rod.
2. Ask them to fly from the high spot to you.
3. If they don't (which they probably won't), grab a chair and stand on the chair. Ask them to fly down a short amount to you, using all the millet rewards etc. from the above steps.
4. Eventually, go lower until you're on the ground. Repeat high-low recall training over several days until your bird is super comfortable descending.
