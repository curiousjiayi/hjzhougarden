# Outer Wilds

It's hard to explain why I love Outer Wilds without spoiling the game. In fact, knowing nearly anything about the game spoils the game. Even Redditors, famed for their disregard of other humans, refuse to say anything to a potential player other than "you can only play this game once, just do it."

Please play this game. I think everybody should give it a fair try at least once (3-5 hours of gameplay). But I know that there are a wide variety of gamers, so here are some factors that can guide whether you pick up the game or not:

### Reasons you should vs. shouldn't play Outer Wilds

You will like this game if you:
- **Want to be rewarded for your curiosity and courage.** You know when you talk to kids and they ask: "Why? And then? How? What happened after that?" Outer Wilds is a game where you ask a sequence of these questions one after the other and they lead you to joyous, gut-wrenching, existential, peaceful answers.
- **Like the logical process of traversing knowledge graphs.** Progress in Outer Wilds is made solely in terms of what you know. At first every piece of information you collect lives as an isolated node, which can feel confusing and disconnected; but the first moment you connect an edge between two nodes, a spark lights up and never leaves. Outer Wilds never gives you overt goals, but I wouldn't call it a directionless game. At every point there is _always_ something big to figure out, and this game suits players who like to find those unspoken gravity wells.
- **Want to be impacted by the games you play.** If you like story-rich games, Outer Wilds is one of the best games you can play. If you like randomly bursting into tears at 10AM the morning after finishing a game, even better!


You will not like this game if you:
- **Need linear progression and upgrades.** There are no skill trees, upgrades, levels, items, or any other markers of progress in Outer Wilds. The only progression made is in _knowledge_. If you know the story and know how to execute it, you could complete the game in less than fifteen minutes (but of course this is not the point of the game). The progression is the friends we made along the way, literally.
- **Don't like to waste your time.** There are a lot of runs that can be described objectively as "a waste of time", and metaphorically as "making peace with endings."
- **Don't like puzzle games.** People shy away from describing Outer Wilds because they don't want to box such an expansive game into labels. While I agree with that, Outer Wilds is at its core a narrative-driven puzzle game. It might not be a traditional puzzle, but if you don't like solving mysteries, you won't like Outer Wilds.

- The first run I made it into space, in which I decided to visit Giant's Deep (gotta go to the biggest planet in the solar system, obviously), sat in orbit around the planet wondering where I should land, got surprise manhandled by the O****** S**** P****, crashed into the ocean, and stared at the massive water tornadoes while treading water until I died. 
- Blasting from volcano hole to volcano hole in Timber Hearth. "Ohh my god it's little tadpole Hearthians!" I would say, right as I get sucked up by superheated waters and farted into the sky just to come tumbling down into the shaft of another volcano.
- Auto-piloting into the Sun, rebirthing, and immediately autopiloting into the Sun again (a rite of passage).
- Cursing my sweaty, fumbling fingers as I frantically right myself in the T**** of Q****** K******** only to overshoot and miss the keyhole for the scroll and that blasted ending song grows louder and louder and louder
- Opening the door of the Shrine at the S**** L******* on the Q****** M*** and gripping my controller as I saw the road unfold in front of me. Meeting S******. I was losing it.
